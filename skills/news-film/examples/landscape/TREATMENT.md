## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Round-up with one item**: sting → rundown of one row → item → rundown ticked. A rundown with a single row is empty ceremony.
2. **Cold open on the quote → sting → one lead package → stamp freezes into the end card.** The King's strongest line opens the film as a hook, the package explains where, when and why, the quote returns in full, and the closing line ("learn from the past") ends it. The first and last frames are both his words.
3. **Countdown of three stops** (Bahamas 3, Guyana 2, Antigua 1): a list that counts down to the speech. It makes the tour the story and the speech a footnote; the speech is the news.

**Chosen: 2.** It differs from the demo grammar (round-up), puts the hook in the first second, and lets the map long take be the bulletin's breath between two quote moments.

## 2. Logline and arc

At the opening of the 28th Commonwealth summit in Antigua, at the end of a three-nation Caribbean tour, King Charles III says the transatlantic slave trade fills him with "the most profound personal sorrow" — and asks the Commonwealth to learn from the past.

- Setup: the line, cold (0–3 s). The bulletin opens; the map traces the tour.
- Turn: the scale of the room he spoke to (a third of humanity, more than a billion young people), then one beat of silence and the full quote.
- Ending: "We can, however, learn from the past." The one stamp lands, the frame freezes and slants into the end card.

## 3. Benchmark

- Evening network news lead packages on a head of state's speech abroad; European news channels' explainer maps for travel routes.
- Learn: one fact per screen; the map as a long take with the route drawn in order; the quote card where the words arrive in phrases and one word is underlined; restraint on a grave subject (no stingers on the slavery line).
- Don't take: any broadcaster's logo, set, idents, colours, fonts or music; no photos of the King (none we have rights to); no flags.
